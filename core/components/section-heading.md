### Hierarchy

What each `Hierarchy` value is for. The words, the rules every component keeps and the owner's calls H1–H4 are in [hierarchy.md](../hierarchy.md); this section adds Section heading's own. Sources: Uber Base [Section heading, Usage](https://base.uber.com/6d2425e9f/p/5587b7-section-heading/b/46994b), Apple HIG [Lists and tables](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/lists-and-tables) and [Typography](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/typography), read 2026-11-09, and the iOS UI Kit's `Header` (Sources). Gap: Uber's Section heading page has no Content tab, and its Specs tab (Type and color, screen readers) is images only, so no text came through.

A heading's `Hierarchy` is how prominent the heading is, not how important an action is. Uber's Section heading has no Hierarchy axis. It draws one heading, and for a long list of similar items it says to make the headings less prominent by overriding their text style and colour (Customizations › Text styles). The iOS kit's `Header` draws three tiers. Our three values map onto both:

| `Hierarchy` | Use for | Don't | Title style | Uber Base | iOS kit `Header` |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Primary** (default) | The heading of a main section of a scrolling screen, above a list, cards or tiles, often with a "see all" Link (Usage: `กองทุนแนะนำ`). Uber places section headings on top of related list items, cards and tiles. | Not a page title: never above a navigation header or in its place (Uber Don't). Not a Tile's title. | `Headings/Title 2` 22/32 Strong, `text-primary` | the default heading (`Heading/Small` 24/32) | Extra Prominent (22/28 Bold) |
| **Secondary** | *Recommendation*: a section one step below a Primary on the same screen, or a screen's sections when they aren't its main content (Usage: `การแจ้งเตือน` with its Description). No source says what this middle level is for. The iOS kit's Prominent tier is the only basis. | Not to make one group stand out from sibling groups (see the consistency rule below). | `Headings/Title 3` 20/28 Strong, `text-primary` | a less prominent override (no named style) | Prominent (20/25 Semibold) |
| **Tertiary** | A long run of similar groups, such as the groups on a settings screen, each above its Grouped list (Usage: `บัญชี`). This is Uber's less prominent heading for a long list of similar items. Apple's grouped style also separates groups with headers. Also a heading nested inside a Primary or Secondary section (the kit's Nested). | Don't mix it with Secondary on sibling groups of the same kind. | `Headings/Headline` 17/24 Medium, `text-secondary` | the less prominent override, in text style and colour | Nested (17/22 Semibold, `Labels/Secondary`) |

#### Rules

- **Sibling headings take one `Hierarchy`.** Headings over similar content use the same value (Uber Don't: no inconsistent custom text styles across section headings with similar content).
- **Nest by stepping down one value.** A section inside a Primary section takes Secondary, and one inside that takes Tertiary. A screen needn't use all three. *Recommendation*, from the iOS kit's three tiers. No source states the step.
- **Several Primaries on one screen are fine**, one per main section. [Rule 1](../hierarchy.md) counts actions, not headings ([H5](../hierarchy.md)): a heading's `Hierarchy` is visual emphasis.
- **The order holds at every text size.** Accessibility item 6 checks the reflow. Primary and Secondary are Strong because Apple's bold trait adds a level of hierarchy.
- **Title copy**: descriptive, so people can find what they need (Uber, When to use). *Recommendation*: a noun or short noun phrase that names the group, with no ending punctuation. That is Apple's rule for column headings (Lists and tables), carried over to titles. The `_Column label`s follow Apple's rule directly. Title-style capitals apply to English copy only.

#### Nested parts

- **Icon button** (`Trailing=Button`) is pinned to `Hierarchy=Secondary`, `Type=Default`, `Size=Small` in all three `Hierarchy` rows; the heading's value doesn't change it. This follows [H3](../hierarchy.md): an Icon button is Secondary unless its container draws its own surface. Section heading has no fill (Assumptions), so a Tertiary one would be a bare glyph. Uber lists an icon button as trailing content, and Uber's Button page makes icon buttons Secondary. Rule 3 holds too.
- **Link** (`Trailing=Link`) is pinned to `Style=Primary`, `Size=Medium`. `Style` is Link's own colour axis, not a Button `Hierarchy`, so it doesn't count toward rule 1's one Primary (inference: hierarchy.md doesn't name Link).
- Disclosure, Image and Column labels have no `Hierarchy`.
